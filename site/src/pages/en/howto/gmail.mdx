## Why Gmail

Buying a server, signing up for GitHub and other overseas services all need an email address that receives mail reliably. Chinese mailboxes work too, but some overseas services' emails get blocked or delayed. Gmail is the least hassle for overseas services.

<Callout type="tip">
Opening Google's sites needs a network that can reach them. No line yet? Buy the server with your existing email first, and create Gmail after installing the apps in step 4.
</Callout>

## Steps

1. Open [accounts.google.com/signup](https://accounts.google.com/signup) (or gmail.com → "Create account").
2. Choose "For my personal use".
3. Enter your name, birthday and gender. Use your real birthday — under-age accounts are restricted.
4. Pick a Gmail address, e.g. `yourname2026@gmail.com`. Taken? Try another.
5. Set a password: at least 12 characters mixing letters, numbers and symbols, not reused elsewhere.
6. Verify your phone number when asked (see below).
7. Add a recovery email so you can get back in if you forget the password.

## Right after signing up: turn on 2-Step Verification

1. Open [myaccount.google.com](https://myaccount.google.com) and click "Security".
2. Find "2-Step Verification" and turn it on — an authenticator app or "Google prompts" are recommended.

Then even a leaked password won't let anyone into your account.

<Callout type="ok">
You can log in to Gmail with the new address and send yourself a test email; "Security" shows 2-Step Verification is on.
</Callout>

## Already have Gmail and want a different address

Google now lets you **change an existing account to a new @gmail.com address**, without signing up again:

1. Open [myaccount.google.com/google-account-email](https://myaccount.google.com/google-account-email) (or in your Google Account: Personal info → Email → Google Account email).
2. If your account is eligible, you'll see an option to change the address. Enter the new address and check it's available.
3. Confirm the change as prompted.

Things to know:

- **Once a year, at most three times in total**, and you can switch back to a previous address.
- **The old address keeps working**: it becomes an alias, and both addresses receive mail. Your mail, Drive and contacts stay in the same account.
- **It changes the address; it doesn't add a second mailbox**: old and new addresses belong to the same Google account.
- **Region**: it launched in the US first in 2026 and is rolling out elsewhere. If you don't see the option, your account can't use it yet — wait, or create a new account.

<Callout type="tip" title="Change the address, or create a new account?">
Just want a nicer, easier-to-remember address? Change it. Need a **completely separate** mailbox — for example, one used only for a non-China Apple ID, kept apart from your main account? **Create a new account** with the steps above.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warn">
Before changing, think about which sites you signed up to with the old address. It still receives mail, but some sites may keep asking you to log in with the old address.
</Callout>

## Stuck?

<Q q="“This phone number cannot be used for verification”?">
That's Google's risk control, common on new devices and networks. Try again later, or sign up in the Gmail app on your phone, which usually works better.
</Q>
<Q q="No verification SMS?">
Wait a minute or two before resending, and check the country code (mainland China is +86).
</Q>
<Q q="The new account was disabled right away?">
Frequent IP changes or bulk actions on a new account trigger risk control. Appeal as prompted; it settles down after a period of normal use.
</Q>
